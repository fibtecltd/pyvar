How the portal assistant fits together

One layout, drawn for each tier. Menu questions never reach a model at request time: they are static files holding answers that were checked once, offline. The tier only changes who writes and who checks the typed, open-ended answers.

Everything measured so far is about fixed menu actions (explain, when to use, compare) written by Haiku. Typed questions and the larger models are drawn here but not measured yet.

What changed since the first sketch.

  1. Menu answers are static files on the CDN, so the gateway, the quota and the router are gone from that path. The widget picks menu or typed. This is faster, free, and leaves nothing to abuse.
  2. The watchdog audits by a daily budget spread over functions, not a percentage. At 1%, one function would need about 2,000 live answers to fill a 20-answer window, so a per-function rule would almost never fire.
  3. The pin is a flag the gateway reads, not a router decision.

Decisions

Settled

  1. Menu answers come from a stored bank written by Haiku with the v4 prompt, checked by the graders then Opus, and served as static files. Same bank in every tier.
  2. No more than 4% of published answers may carry a wrong claim, and no person sits in the request path (Filippo).
  3. Static text built from the record is the floor: a second failed check, or a pinned function.
  4. Typed questions use a live agent with read-only tools. It never computes a number, gives advice or handles a key.
  5. The ladder Haiku, then Sonnet or Opus, then Opus or Fable is the target. A larger model is switched on only after it is measured.
  6. The spare reading batch is skipped (Filippo).

Starting values, to tune on real traffic

  1. Audit budget about $2 a day, shared by free and pro, spread so every function with traffic gets audited answers.
  2. Pin a function when 3 of its last 20 audited answers are flagged.
  3. Alert when more than 8% of the last 200 audited answers are flagged.
  4. One retry, then static text.
  5. The 8 cases flagged on two or more draws start as static text until their record is fixed.
  6. Quotas from the plan: anonymous 10 an hour per IP, free 40 a day, pro 200, enterprise 500.

Open, needs a decision (my pick first)

  1. Typed questions in the first release? No: ship the bank first, then typed questions after the measurement run.
  2. Who regenerates the bank when the catalogue changes? A test fails CI until it is regenerated; an automated job that opens the PR is my pick, and it needs the Anthropic key in CI.
  3. Anonymous visitors on the live agent? Keep 10 an hour per IP: the bank covers the common case.
  4. Fable: price and availability are unknown.
  5. Does a person still read a sample of the bank at every release? The rule behind "Opus is usable as the gate" assumed it: 40 random answers, about half an hour, outside the request path. Yes, keep it: nothing else measures what slips past Opus, and without it the only bound is the 8.8% above.

Where the human went

Done once, by a person: a blind read (the model's text was preferred in 11 of 12 pairs) and the recall check that found 0 errors in 40 answers Opus passed. That is how we know the Opus check can be trusted at all.

Not replaced yet: the measured design had a person read at least 40 bank answers at every release. The watchdog watches live typed answers only, so nothing automatic takes that job for the bank. Without it, all we can say about what slips past Opus is the 8.8% bound at 95% confidence, above the 4% tolerance. See the last open question.
